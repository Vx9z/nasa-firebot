# 2.1 What changes in microgravity
**Module:** 2 — Microgravity Combustion · **Prev:** [1.6](1.6-soot-combustion-products.md) · **Next:** [2.2](2.2-flame-shape-color-temperature.md)

## Level 1 — Overview
Removing buoyancy fundamentally changes combustion — transport becomes diffusion-dominated.
- Near-zero g minimizes buoyant flows → diffusion-dominated transport. `[14, 27]`
- Phenomena hidden on Earth by gravity become visible/isolated in µg. `[14]`

## Level 2 — Detail
On Earth, hot gas rises, pulling fresh air into a flame and carrying products away. In microgravity that entire engine stops: no natural convection, no upward draft. Fuel and oxygen can only meet by slow molecular diffusion, and products linger instead of being swept away.
- With no natural convection, transport is by slow molecular diffusion — flames draw fuel/oxidizer together diffusively. `[14, 27]`
- Weak forces normally masked by gravity (capillary, radiation, thermophoresis) become visible. `[14]`
- This "idealizes" combustion — isolating phenomena for cleaner scientific study. `[14]`
- Soot, flame shape, and spread all change because buoyancy no longer sweeps products away. `[14, 27]`

## Level 3 — Deep dive
Why do we study combustion in space at all? Because gravity *hides* physics. Buoyancy is so strong on Earth that it masks the subtler forces — diffusion, radiation, surface tension — that actually govern combustion at a fundamental level. Remove gravity and you get a "clean" view of combustion, where a droplet burns as a perfect sphere and a flame spreads purely by heat conduction and diffusion. That idealized view is what lets researchers build correct models and then re-apply them to the messy, buoyant flames of Earth — and to fire safety in spacecraft. Microgravity isn't an exotic curiosity; it's the cleanest laboratory for the physics that underpins every fire.
