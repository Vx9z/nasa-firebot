## Level 1 — Overview
Fire safety for long-duration missions — Moon, Mars, and beyond.
- Spacecraft Fire Safety Technology Development Plan (2020) is the roadmap. `[60]`
- Fire-safety needs for exploration missions and habitats. `[61, 63]`

## Level 2 — Detail
As missions get longer and farther from Earth, fire safety gets harder. A Moon or Mars habitat can't rely on quick rescue or a nearby resupply — a fire is a mission- and life-threatening event that must be prevented and handled entirely on-site. NASA has a formal technology development plan for exactly this.
- The 2020 Technology Development Plan lays out fire-safety tech needs for exploration missions. `[60]`
- Fire during a far-from-Earth mission is a critical risk for vehicles & habitats. `[61, 63]`
- Solid-fuel ignition testing is being extended to lunar applications. `[64]`
- Long-duration Moon/Mars missions raise the stakes (no quick rescue, limited resources). `[61]`

## Level 3 — Deep dive
The Moon/Mars push changes the fire-safety problem in kind, not just degree. On the ISS, a fire means "grab the extinguisher, and Earth is 400 km away with a resupply schedule." On Mars, there's no quick return, no emergency escape, and every gram of suppression agent had to be launched months earlier. So the emphasis shifts hard toward *prevention and prediction* — choose materials that won't burn, model every scenario, and design systems that fail safe. That's why SoFIE is already testing solid-fuel ignition for *lunar* applications, and why the 2020 plan reads like a roadmap for a self-sufficient fire-safety capability. This is the "so what" of the entire curriculum: everything we've learned feeds into keeping humans alive on another world.
