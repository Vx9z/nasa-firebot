# 5.2 Fire safety systems (FPDS)

## Level 1 — Overview
Fire safety is layered: prevent → detect → suppress.
- FPDS = Fire Prevention, Detection & Suppression for exploration vehicles/habitats. `[30]`
- Safety layered: prevention (materials, fusing) → detection → suppression. `[28, 30]`

## Level 2 — Detail
A robust fire-safety system works in layers. The first line is prevention — choose safe materials, limit wire currents, use fuses and grounding so nothing overheats. The second is detection — smoke and gas sensors (Module 3). The last resort is suppression — extinguishers. FPDS formalizes all three for exploration missions.
- First line of defense is prevention: strict material selection, low wire-current ratings, fusing, grounding. `[28]`
- Detection is the second line (smoke/heat/gas signatures). `[28]`
- Suppression is the last line (extinguishers). `[28]`
- FPDS formalizes these three layers for exploration vehicles/habitats. `[30]`

## Level 3 — Deep dive
Defense-in-depth is the philosophy: no single layer has to be perfect if they work together. Prevention stops most fires before they start (the cheapest, most reliable layer). Detection catches the ones that slip through, early enough to act. Suppression is the emergency fallback. FPDS (Fire Prevention, Detection & Suppression) is NASA's program that coordinates R&D across all three, because a space fire is too dangerous to rely on any one line of defense. This layered thinking is also exactly how you'd design the *software* for a dashboard — prevent bad outcomes, detect problems early, suppress damage.
