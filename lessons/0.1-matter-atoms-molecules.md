# 0.1 Matter, atoms & molecules
**Module:** 0 — Foundations · **Prev:** — · **Next:** [0.2](0.2-chemical-reactions-energy.md)

## Level 1 — Overview
Matter is made of atoms that bond into molecules; combustion is just a rearrangement of those bonds.
- Atoms are the building blocks; elements combine into molecules via chemical bonds. 
- A molecule's structure determines how it reacts. 

## Level 2 — Detail
Everything around us — fuel, air, water — is atoms joined into molecules. Combustion takes the atoms of a fuel and an oxidizer and re-arranges them into new molecules (CO₂, H₂O), releasing the energy that was holding the original bonds together.
- An atom = a nucleus (protons + neutrons) + orbiting electrons; the proton count defines the element. 
- Bonds are covalent (shared electrons) or ionic (transferred electrons); bond strength = stored energy. 
- Combustion breaks reactant bonds and forms product bonds — the net difference is the heat released. 

## Level 3 — Deep dive
Why do different fuels release different amounts of heat? It comes down to bond energies. Strong bonds store more energy; when combustion replaces them with even stronger product bonds (like C=O and O–H), the excess is released as heat. Hydrogen's H–H bond and carbon's C–C/C–H bonds are the main energy stores in fuels — this is why hydrogen burns hot and why hydrocarbons (methane, kerosene, plastics) are energy-dense. This one idea — "rearrange bonds, release the difference" — is the foundation underneath every later lesson on flames, soot, and fire safety.
