# 2.6 Solid fuel combustion (BASS/SoFIE)
**Module:** 2 — Microgravity Combustion · **Prev:** [2.5](2.5-gas-phase-flames.md) · **Next:** [2.7](2.7-smoldering.md)

## Level 1 — Overview
Burning solid materials — the BASS and SoFIE programs map how solids ignite and extinguish in space.
- BASS/BASS-II studied burning & suppression of solids on ISS. `[31, 32]`
- SoFIE (Solid Fuel Ignition & Extinction) extends solid-fuel work, incl. lunar applications. `[37, 64]`

## Level 2 — Detail
Solids — fabrics, plastics, insulation — are the real fire load inside a spacecraft, so their behavior matters most for safety. BASS burned solid fuels to study suppression, and SoFIE continues that with controlled ignition/extinction tests. Supporting experiments (SIBAL, FLARE) map the flammability boundary.
- BASS burned solid fuels (fabrics) to study suppression in µg. `[31, 32]`
- SoFIE studies solid-fuel ignition and extinction on the ISS. `[37]`
- SIBAL defines the solid inflammability boundary at low-speed flow. `[15]`
- FLARE measures flammability limits at reduced gravity for everyday materials. `[36]`
- PMMA: thickness and preheat shift flammability; silicone depends on buoyant conditions. `[33, 34, 45]`

## Level 3 — Deep dive
Solid fuels are the most *practical* microgravity combustion topic because they're what a spacecraft is made of — wire insulation, panels, fabrics, cargo. BASS (Burning and Suppression of Solids) and its successor SoFIE (Solid Fuel Ignition and Extinction) exist to answer: which solids ignite, how fast do they burn, and what puts them out in microgravity? The results are sobering — materials behave differently in µg, and the terrestrial flammability tests don't always transfer. SoFIE's extension to lunar applications shows the direction: as we design Moon and Mars habitats, we need solid-fuel fire data that's valid in *their* reduced gravity, not just Earth's.
