# 4.3 Combustion products & acid gases
**Module:** 4 — Materials, Toxicity & Standards · **Prev:** [4.2](4.2-toxic-hazardous-products.md) · **Next:** [4.4](4.4-flammability-testing-standards.md)

## Level 1 — Overview
The full chemical fallout of a fire — and cleaning it up afterward.
- Combustion products from large-scale fires are evaluated for hazard. `[48]`
- Acid-gas interactions and HCl chemistry are studied for post-fire cleanup. `[54, 38]`

## Level 2 — Detail
A fire doesn't end when the flame goes out — the combustion products linger. Saffire-style large-scale tests measure what a real fire releases, so we know what a crew would face. Acid gases (like HCl) are a particular problem because they're corrosive and cling to surfaces.
- Saffire offgas testing strategy quantifies the combustion products of large fires. `[46]`
- HCl (hydrogen chloride) is a major acid-gas product that interacts with materials. `[51, 54]`
- Hazardous-product detection and environmental clean-up are part of post-fire response. `[38]`

## Level 3 — Deep dive
After a fire in a sealed spacecraft, you have two problems: what's in the air, and what's stuck to the walls. The Saffire offgas program measured the *products* of realistic large-scale fires — soot, CO, HCl, and other species — giving engineers real numbers for how bad a fire's "aftermath" is. Acid gases like HCl are the sneaky part: they dissolve into moisture and react with surfaces, corroding equipment and lingering long after the smoke clears. That's why "hazardous product detection and environmental clean-up" is a distinct research area — putting out the flame is only the beginning of the response.
