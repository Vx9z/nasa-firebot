# 2.7 Smoldering
**Module:** 2 — Microgravity Combustion · **Prev:** [2.6](2.6-solid-fuel-combustion.md) · **Next:** [3.1](3.1-fire-signatures.md)

## Level 1 — Overview
Smoldering is slow, non-flaming combustion — and a hidden fire hazard.
- Smoldering = low-temperature, non-flaming reaction; controlled by heat loss. `[16]`
- In µg, reduced heat loss (no convection) → slightly higher smolder temperatures. `[16]`

## Level 2 — Detail
Not all fire has flames. Smoldering is a slow, self-sustaining, flameless reaction inside porous solids (foam, insulation, upholstery) — think of a cigarette or a slowly glowing ember. It's driven by heat, not flames, and it's governed by how fast heat leaks away.
- Smoldering is a self-sustaining, flameless reaction in porous solids (foam, insulation). `[16]`
- It's controlled by heat losses to the surroundings. `[16]`
- In µg, less convective heat loss → slightly higher smolder temperatures than on Earth. `[16]`
- Hazard: smoldering can transition to open flaming — a spacecraft fire risk. `[28]`

## Level 3 — Deep dive
Smoldering is the fire you *don't* see — which is exactly why it's dangerous in a spacecraft. It produces smoke and toxic products without a visible flame, can smolder for a long time hidden inside material, and can suddenly *transition to flaming* if conditions change. The USML-1 shuttle experiments showed that in microgravity, smoldering runs slightly hotter because there's no convection to carry heat away — meaning the balance of heat generation vs. loss shifts in space. For fire detection (Module 3), smoldering is a nightmare: no flame signature, weak heat signature, mostly smoke. That's why smoke detection is the first line of defense.
