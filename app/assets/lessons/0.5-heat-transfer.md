# 0.5 Heat transfer: conduction, convection, radiation
**Module:** 0 — Foundations · **Prev:** [0.4](0.4-density-buoyancy.md) · **Next:** [0.6](0.6-gravity-microgravity.md)

## Level 1 — Overview
Heat moves three ways, and which dominates depends on gravity.
- Conduction (contact), convection (fluid motion), radiation (electromagnetic). 
- In microgravity, convection nearly vanishes → radiation and diffusion dominate. `[14]`

## Level 2 — Detail
Heat always flows from hot to cold, by three mechanisms. Conduction is molecule-to-molecule contact. Convection is heat carried by a moving fluid (rising hot air). Radiation is electromagnetic emission — and it doesn't need any medium at all.
- Conduction: heat through contact between molecules (Fourier's law). 
- Convection: heat carried by moving fluid — natural (buoyancy-driven) vs. forced (fan/flow). 
- Radiation: electromagnetic emission, strongly temperature-dependent (∝ T⁴); soot makes flames strongly radiating. `[14]`
- In µg, natural convection disappears, so radiation + molecular diffusion control heat. `[14]`

## Level 3 — Deep dive
Why does this matter for space fires? Because the *balance* of heat transfer changes. On Earth, convection carries heat away efficiently. In microgravity there's no natural convection, so a flame can only lose heat by conduction (slow) and radiation. This is why microgravity flames are *cooler* — radiation dominates and radiates energy away — and why they're dimmer and harder to detect (Module 3). The "∝ T⁴" law also means sooty (radiating) flames cool especially fast, which links directly to soot behavior in space.
