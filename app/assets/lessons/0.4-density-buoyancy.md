# 0.4 Density & buoyancy (fluids)
**Module:** 0 — Foundations · **Prev:** [0.3](0.3-gases-pressure-temperature-density.md) · **Next:** [0.5](0.5-heat-transfer.md)

## Level 1 — Overview
Density differences in a gravity field create buoyant force and natural convection.
- Hot (less-dense) gas rises; cool (denser) gas sinks → natural convection. 
- Buoyancy is behind upward flames, flame flicker, and hot-gas plumes on Earth. `[14]`

## Level 2 — Detail
Buoyancy is the upward force on something lighter than its surroundings. When a flame heats air, that air expands and becomes less dense, so surrounding denser air pushes it upward — this is natural convection, and it's what shapes every flame you've ever seen on Earth.
- Archimedes' principle: an upward buoyant force equals the weight of displaced fluid. 
- Thermal expansion lowers density → hot parcels rise, cool parcels sink → convection cells. 
- On Earth, buoyancy gives flames their teardrop shape, flicker, and soot-tipped yellow tip. `[14]`
- Buoyancy strength scales with gravity (g) — remove g and this whole mechanism disappears. `[14]`

## Level 3 — Deep dive
Buoyancy isn't just a "flame looks this way" detail — it *drives* the physics. On Earth, buoyancy pulls fresh cool air into the flame bottom and flings hot products out the top, feeding the flame and carrying soot upward. It also causes the flicker of a candle. Take gravity away and none of that happens: no draft, no flicker, no upward flame — transport becomes molecular diffusion alone. That single removal is the whole reason microgravity combustion (Module 2) looks and behaves so differently.
