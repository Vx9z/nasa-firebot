# 0.3 Gases: pressure, temperature, density
**Module:** 0 — Foundations · **Prev:** [0.2](0.2-chemical-reactions-energy.md) · **Next:** [0.4](0.4-density-buoyancy.md)

## Level 1 — Overview
How gases behave links temperature, pressure, and density — hotter gas is less dense.
- Ideal gas: density ρ = P / (R·T) — hotter gas is less dense at constant pressure. 
- This density↔temperature link drives buoyancy and flame rise on Earth. `[14]`

## Level 2 — Detail
A gas's density isn't fixed — it depends on pressure and temperature. Heat a gas at constant pressure and it expands, becoming lighter per unit volume. This is the root cause of buoyancy: hot combustion products are lighter than the cool air around them, so they rise.
- Ideal gas law: PV = nRT; density form ρ = PM/(RT) where M = molar mass. 
- At constant pressure, hotter gas is less dense (lighter) than cooler gas. 
- Hot combustion products are lighter than surrounding air → they rise (buoyancy). `[14]`
- Partial pressure: each gas in a mixture contributes its own pressure — relevant to oxygen fraction. 

## Level 3 — Deep dive
The density–temperature link is the single most important "why" for this entire curriculum. On Earth, a flame is a column of hot, low-density gas pushing upward through cooler, denser air — that's the teardrop shape, the flicker, and the upward draft. In microgravity (Module 2), the same gas has no "up" to rise into, so the whole buoyancy-driven structure vanishes and flames become spheres. If you understand this one equation, you understand the difference between fire on Earth and fire in space.
