# 0.2 Chemical reactions & energy
**Module:** 0 — Foundations · **Prev:** [0.1](0.1-matter-atoms-molecules.md) · **Next:** [0.3](0.3-gases-pressure-temperature-density.md)

## Level 1 — Overview
A reaction turns reactants into products, releasing or absorbing energy; combustion is an exothermic reaction.
- Combustion: fuel + oxidizer → products + heat. 
- Reactions need activation energy to start; exothermic reactions release heat. 

## Level 2 — Detail
Chemical reactions rearrange atoms and either release energy (exothermic) or absorb it (endothermic). Combustion is strongly exothermic — that's why fire is hot. Every reaction needs a "push" to get going (activation energy), but once an exothermic reaction starts, it sustains itself.
- Exothermic = releases heat; endothermic = absorbs heat. 
- Activation energy is the initial barrier; once crossed, exothermic reactions self-sustain. 
- Chain reactions: reactive intermediates (radicals) regenerate themselves — the "chain reaction" side of the fire tetrahedron. 

## Level 3 — Deep dive
The self-sustaining nature of fire comes from a chain reaction. In a flame, heat breaks molecules into reactive fragments (free radicals like H, O, OH). These radicals react with fuel, producing more radicals plus heat — which produces yet more radicals. It's a positive feedback loop that keeps the flame alive as long as fuel, oxidizer, and heat are present. This is exactly why the fire *tetrahedron* (Module 1) adds "chain reaction" to the classic fire triangle: you can't fully explain a flame without radical chemistry.
