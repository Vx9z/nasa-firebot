# 5.4 Modeling & prediction
**Module:** 5 — Spacecraft Fire Safety & Exploration · **Prev:** [5.3](5.3-saffire-experiments.md) · **Next:** [5.5](5.5-exploration-moon-mars.md)

## Level 1 — Overview
Turning experiments into predictive capability — modeling spacecraft fires.
- Vehicle modeling of large-scale fires; a "verified Saffire model" enables fire-safety predictions. `[53, 55, 57, 58]`

## Level 2 — Detail
You can't fly an experiment for every possible fire scenario, so you build computer models instead. These simulate how fire grows and spreads inside a spacecraft, and they're validated ("verified") against real data — especially Saffire's — so they can be trusted to predict fires you haven't tested.
- Model development of large-scale spacecraft fires during Saffire. `[53]`
- Vehicle modeling of burn events (thermally-thick samples, Saffire VI). `[55, 57]`
- A verified Saffire model enables spacecraft fire-safety predictions. `[58]`
- Goal: predict fire behavior without flying every configuration. `[58]`

## Level 3 — Deep dive
Modeling is how fire safety *scales*. Experiments are expensive and limited; models let you explore "what if" — what if this material ignites in this configuration at this oxygen level? The Saffire-era work produced a "verified" model: one that has been checked against enough real fire data that its predictions are trusted. That's a huge milestone, because it means future spacecraft (Artemis, Mars habitats) can be designed with fire-safety simulations rather than by guessing. The loop is: experiment → model → verify → predict → design. It's the difference between "we hope this is safe" and "we've computed it's safe."
