# 3.2 Smoke detection

## Level 1 — Overview
Smoke detectors are the workhorse of fire detection, tuned to balance speed against false alarms.
- Detector choice balances earliest warning vs. avoiding false alarms. `[28]`
- Smoke particulate characterization (NASA + NIST) refines sensitivity & alarm criteria. `[29]`

## Level 2 — Detail
Smoke detectors sense airborne particles. The two main kinds are photoelectric (a light beam gets scattered by smoke) and ionization (smoke changes an electric current). Spacecraft detectors are adaptations of these terrestrial designs, retuned for the smoke that microgravity fires actually produce.
- Two main types: photoelectric (light scattering) vs. ionization (current change). 
- Spacecraft smoke detectors are adaptations of terrestrial designs. `[28]`
- Smoke density & soot characteristics from µg fires improve sensitivity and alarm criteria. `[29, 28]`
- Avoiding false alarms is critical (false alarms disrupt crew). `[28]`

## Level 3 — Deep dive
Why retune smoke detectors for space? Because the *smoke itself is different*. Microgravity soot agglomerates into larger, different-structured particles (Lesson 1.6), which scatter light differently than Earth-fire smoke. NASA and NIST collaborated on "smoke particulate characterization" specifically to measure these differences and set alarm thresholds that catch real fires without crying wolf. False alarms aren't trivial — on a spacecraft, every alarm triggers crew action and potential mission disruption. So a spacecraft smoke detector is a carefully-tuned instrument, not an off-the-shelf home unit.
