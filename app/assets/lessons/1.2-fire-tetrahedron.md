# 1.2 The fire tetrahedron
**Module:** 1 — Combustion & Fire · **Prev:** [1.1](1.1-what-is-combustion.md) · **Next:** [1.3](1.3-flame-anatomy-types.md)

## Level 1 — Overview
Four elements must coexist for fire to sustain itself — remove one and it stops.
- Fuel + oxidizer + heat + chain reaction = the fire tetrahedron. `[14, 28]`
- Remove any one element → fire stops (the basis of all suppression). `[28]`

## Level 2 — Detail
The fire tetrahedron names the four things a fire needs at once: fuel (something to burn), oxidizer (usually oxygen), heat (to sustain ignition), and the chain reaction (the self-propagating radical chemistry). This model is the conceptual key to all fire safety — every extinguisher works by removing one of the four.
- Each suppression method removes one side: cool (remove heat), starve (remove fuel), inert (remove oxidizer), inhibit (break the chain reaction). `[28, 14]`
- Chemical agents like Halon break the chain reaction rather than removing heat/oxygen. `[14]`
- The tetrahedron adds "chain reaction" to the older fire triangle, capturing radical chemistry. 

## Level 3 — Deep dive
The "chain reaction" side is the subtle one and the reason modern suppression is so effective. A flame isn't just hot fuel — it's a cascade of free radicals (H, O, OH) that regenerate themselves. Halon-type agents don't cool or smother the fire; they *scavenge* those radicals, shutting the reaction down chemically even at low concentrations. This is why understanding the tetrahedron isn't just theory — it explains *why different extinguishers exist* (water cools, CO₂ smothers, Halon inhibits) and why a fire in space needs different agents than one on Earth (Module 3).
