# 5.3 Saffire experiments (I–VI)
**Module:** 5 — Spacecraft Fire Safety & Exploration · **Prev:** [5.2](5.2-fire-safety-systems.md) · **Next:** [5.4](5.4-modeling-prediction.md)

## Level 1 — Overview
Saffire: the large-scale fire experiments that anchor modern spacecraft fire safety.
- Saffire flew real fires on uncrewed Cygnus vehicles after ISS departure. `[39, 40, 41, 42]`
- Results give fire-growth, flammability, and product data for realistic scales. `[47, 56, 59]`

## Level 2 — Detail
You can't safely light a big fire on the ISS — so NASA lit them on empty cargo vehicles instead. Saffire (Spacecraft Fire Experiment) ignited controlled fires inside a Cygnus cargo ship *after* it undocked and left the station, measuring how real materials burn at realistic scales in microgravity.
- Objective: reduce uncertainty/risk in spacecraft fire-safety design for exploration missions. `[41]`
- Saffire I–VI ran increasingly capable tests (materials, configurations, products). `[39–47, 56]`
- Saffire VI (2024) is the freshest result set. `[56]`
- Heat & smoke emission from Saffire VI tests quantified. `[59]`

## Level 3 — Deep dive
Saffire is the missing link between bench-scale tests and real spacecraft fires. Before Saffire, fire-safety data came from tiny lab flames and theory; nobody had burned a *large* fire in real microgravity. Saffire changed that — it flew six missions (I through VI) with increasingly realistic materials and configurations, measuring flame growth, heat release, smoke, and products. Those results directly validate the computer models (Lesson 5.4) and the flammability standards (Lesson 4.4). When people talk about "modern spacecraft fire safety," they're talking about the Saffire-era data — it's the empirical foundation everything else rests on.
