## Layer 2 — Fire Safety (application focus)

### 2.1 Fire detection
- Purpose of a fire detector: give the **earliest possible warning** of an unwanted fire, **balanced against avoiding false alarms**. `[CP-2476]`
- Detectors respond to **fire signatures** — environmental departures from normal conditions that indicate fire, classified into **heat, smoke, and light (radiation)** categories. `[CP-2476]`
- Detector selection must account for (a) response to the signature and (b) the **time-dependent transport** of signature mass/energy to the detector — plus application cost and response. `[CP-2476]`
- **Analytical fire-growth modeling** is invaluable for matching detectors to anticipated fire hazards. `[CP-2476]`

### 2.2 Standards & testing
- Historically, flammability tests were **empirical**, not rooted in fire-science understanding. `[CP-2476]`
- New-generation **bench-scale tests** are based on physical & chemical phenomena, designed for systematic, standardized prediction of **full-scale fire behavior**. `[CP-2476]`
- New tests evaluate **ignitability, flame spread, and heat & product release**. `[CP-2476]`

### 2.3 Materials, toxicity & hazards
- Spacecraft **polymers** may generate **acids, ammonia, and other toxic products** when heated/burned. `[CP-2476]`
- Additional injuries can come from **overpressure & structural failure** — explosions from igniting reactive gaseous products. `[CP-2476]`
- Toxic-exposure limits should be based on **human mental acuity & judgment** tests, including the **psychology of escape**. `[CP-2476]`

### 2.4 Suppression & inerting
- A fire-safety strategy using an **inert (reduced-oxygen) atmosphere** shows promise, but needs more medical research on human effects in unusual atmospheres. `[CP-2476]` _(inferred — confirm in source)_

### 2.5 Space Station safety concerns
- The proposed U.S. Space Station poses **unique fire-safety problems**: long lifetime, limited resources, hazardous operations, and crew of varying skills. `[CP-2476]`
- Station is made of protected volumes — **habitation, laboratory, and supply modules**. `[CP-2476]`

### 2.6 Aircraft fire safety (analogies for spacecraft)
- Aircraft fire-safety problems are **more diversified** than anticipated in spacecraft, but space can borrow aircraft experience & techniques. `[CP-2476]`
- Major hazard: storage/use of **low-flash-point and kerosene-type fuels**. `[CP-2476]`
- Fuel fires drove work on **fuel-tank protection, armament/explosion protection, heat-absorbing (endothermic) fuels, and improved hydraulic fluids**. `[CP-2476]`
- **Engine compartments/nacelles** use fire detection + **automated extinguishing** systems. `[CP-2476]`
- Aircraft-cabin standards for **extinguishment & escape** are useful analogies for spacecraft fire control. `[CP-2476]`
