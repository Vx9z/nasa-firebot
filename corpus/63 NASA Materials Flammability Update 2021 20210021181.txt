Fire Detection in Lunar - G
♦In terrestrial fires, buoyancy drives smoke upward, allowing convenient placement of smoke detectors on 
ceilings. In microgravity, this buoyant flow does not occur.
♦In lunar gravity (1/6 g), some buoyant flow is expected, but it’s currently unclear how the limited buoyancy 
will affect smoke transport.
♦It is expected that air intakes would be near the floor to capture lunar dust.  Modeling is required to 
determine whether smoke detectors should be at the ceiling or near the air intakes at the floor.
♦This will require good predictions of the potential fire sources

ElectroMotive Drop Tower (EMDT) upgrade proposal
Motivation: Provide inexpensive, reliable and flexible access to 10 s of zero-g or partial-g to facilitate
fundamental research, technology development, and flight hardware risk mitigation testing.
Concept:
Convert the existing 5.2-second GRC Zero Gravity Research Facility to a state-of-the-art ~10-
second, 0 to 0.5g test facility using a vertical rail and linear synchronous motor system to provide a 
revolutionary capability in testing at low and partial gravity at a significantly lower operating cost.
Target Metrics:
10 s duration
10-5 g below 20 Hz
Launch/landing 4 g avg 6 g max
Payload size 1 m x 1 m x 1.8 m
Payload mass 455 kg
20 tests / day (1 OOM increase)
$300 / test (1 OOM reduction)
Applications:
Physical Sciences
o Fundamental Physics
o Combustion
o Fluid Physics
Life Support
o Fire Safety
o Thermal Control
o Fluid Acquisition/Control
o Dust Behavior
Life Science
o Gravitropism
o Plant Thermal Model
ISRU
o Reactor Design
o Regolith Acquisition/ Handling
Science
o Asteroid Sampling
o Soil Physics

Engineering Model Design Complete

years) of engineering model before final
